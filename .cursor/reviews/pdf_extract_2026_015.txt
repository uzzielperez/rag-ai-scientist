Angular analysis of the
B+ →π+µ+µ−decay
LHCb collaboration†
Abstract
This paper presents a first measurement of the angular observables AFB and FH
that parameterise the angular distribution of the B+ →π+µ+µ−decay. The pp
collision dataset used in the analysis corresponds to an integrated luminosity of
9 fb−1, collected with the LHCb experiment between 2011 and 2018. The analysis
is performed in two bins of dimuon invariant-mass squared, q2, one above and one
below the peaks in the q2 distribution from the J/ψ and ψ(2S) narrow charmonium
resonances.
The observables are found to be consistent with Standard Model
predictions.

Introduction
1
The decay B+ →π+µ+µ−is, in the Standard Model of particle physics (SM), mediated by
2
a b- to d-quark flavour-changing neutral-current transition.1 Figure 1 shows the leading-
3
order Feynman diagrams for the B+ →π+µ+µ−decay. The dominant contribution comes
4
from diagrams with an internal t loop due to the large t mass, which enhances its virtual
5
effects in the loop. The small magnitude of the Vtd element of the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–
6
Maskawa (CKM) quark-mixing matrix [1,2] suppresses the branching fraction for the decay
7
to O (10−8). The small SM branching fraction makes the B+ →π+µ+µ−decay sensitive
8
to contributions from particles arising in extensions of the SM. Non-SM contributions
9
could alter observables such as the rate and angular distribution of the decay or their
10
differences between B+ and B−decays. While previous measurements of the branching
11
fraction and CP asymmetry [3,4] are consistent with SM predictions [5–7], the angular
12
distribution of the B+ →π+µ+µ−decay has not previously been measured.
13
The differential decay rate of the B+ →π+µ+µ−decay can be parameterised in terms
14
of the dimuon mass squared, q2, and the angle, θl, between the direction of the µ+ and
15
the direction opposite that of the pion in the dimuon rest frame for the B+ meson decay.
16
Normalising by the total decay rate Γ gives [8,9]

Detector and simulation
38
The LHCb detector [17, 18] is a single-arm forward spectrometer covering the
39
pseudorapidity range 2 < η < 5, designed for the study of particles containing b or
40
c quarks. The detector to collect the data analysed in this paper includes a high-precision
41
tracking system consisting of a silicon-strip vertex detector surrounding the pp interaction
42
region [19], a large-area silicon-strip detector located upstream of a dipole magnet with
43
a bending power of about 4 T m, and three stations of silicon-strip detectors and straw
44
drift tubes [20,21] placed downstream of the magnet. The tracking system provides a
45
measurement of the momentum, p, of charged particles with a relative uncertainty that
46
varies from 0.5% at low momentum to 1.0% at 200 GeV/c. The minimum distance of
47
a track to a primary pp collision vertex (PV), the impact parameter (IP), is measured
48
with a resolution of (15 + 29/pT) µm, where pT is the component of the momentum
49
transverse to the beam, in GeV/c. Different types of charged hadrons are distinguished
50
using information from two ring-imaging Cherenkov detectors [22]. Photons, electrons
51
and hadrons are identified by a calorimeter system consisting of scintillating-pad and
52
preshower detectors, an electromagnetic and a hadronic calorimeter. Muons are identified
53
by a system composed of alternating layers of iron and multiwire proportional cham-
54
bers [23]. The online event selection is performed by a trigger [24], which consists of a
55
hardware stage, based on information from the calorimeter and muon systems, followed
56
by a software stage, which applies a full event reconstruction. Triggered data further
57
undergo a centralised, offline processing step to deliver physics-analysis-ready data across
58
the entire LHCb physics programme [25].
59
Samples of simulated events are used to understand the impact of the detector and
60
subsequent event selection on the angular distribution of the signal B+ →π+µ+µ−decays.
61
They are also used to model the contribution from a variety of backgrounds as described
62
below. In the simulation, pp collisions are generated using Pythia [26] with a specific
63
LHCb configuration [27]. Decays of unstable particles are described by EvtGen [28],
64
in which final-state radiation is generated using Photos [29].
The angular and q2
65
distributions of B+ →π+µ+µ−and B+ →K+µ+µ−decays are generated according to
66
Ref. [9] using form factors from Ref. [30]. The interaction of the generated particles with
67
the detector, and its response, are implemented using the Geant4 toolkit [31] as described
68
in Ref. [32]. The simulated samples are corrected for known data-simulation differences in
69
track reconstruction and muon-identification efficiencies using a tag-and-probe approach
70
with J/ψ →µ+µ−decays. Kaon and pion identification efficiencies are corrected using
71
D∗+ →D0(K−π+)π+ decays, which can be selected in data without particle-identification
72
requirements. Simulated samples are then weighted such that the production kinematics of
73
2

the B+ mesons in the simulation are consistent with the observed momentum distributions
74
of B+ mesons reconstructed through B+ →J/ψK+decays in data.

Candidate selection
76
Events are required to pass a hardware trigger that retains events comprising at least one
77
muon with high transverse momentum, with the minimum threshold varying in the range
78
1200–2200 MeV/c depending on the data-taking period. In the software trigger, at least
79
one of the final-state particles is required to have pT > 0.8 GeV/c and an IP greater than
80
100 µm with respect to all PVs in the event. A multivariate algorithm [33,34] is then used
81
to identify events containing secondary vertices formed by two or three tracks that are
82
consistent with the decay of a b hadron.
83
Offline, B+ candidates are formed from the combination of two oppositely charged
84
tracks that are identified as muons and a third track identified as a pion. The muons are
85
required to have pT > 800 MeV/c. Each track is required to have a good track-fit quality,
86
to be within the acceptance of the particle-identification systems, and have a significant
87
IP with respect to all PVs in the event. The three tracks are required to be consistent
88
with originating from a single point that is displaced from every PV. The B+ candidate is
89
required to have a π+µ+µ−mass in the range 5180 < m(π+µ+µ−) < 5780 MeV/c2 and to
90
be consistent with originating from its associated PV, i.e. the PV with the smallest IP χ2,
91
defined as the difference in the vertex-fit χ2 of the PV reconstructed with and without
92
the candidate.

Angular model
134
In each q2 interval, the observables AFB and FH are determined by performing a simulta-
135
neous unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to the π+µ+µ−mass and cos θl distributions of
136
the selected candidates. The signal angular distribution is modelled by Eq. (1), multiplied
137
by a function describing the angular efficiency of the reconstruction and selection process.
138
The angular resolution of the detector is sufficiently good (better than 1 mrad) that it
139
can be neglected but is considered as a source of systematic uncertainty.
140
Figure 2 shows the efficiency of the reconstruction and selection, without the vetoes
141
applied, as a function of cos θl in the two q2 ranges on simulated B+ →π+µ+µ−decays.
142
The efficiency is well described in each q2 interval by a fourth-order Legendre polynomial
143
model that only includes even-ordered terms and is therefore symmetric around cos θl = 0.
144
The variation of B+ →π+µ+µ−kinematics over q2 causes the selection efficiency of the
145
rare mode to differ between low and high q2 intervals. The main sources of inefficiency
146
are related to the muons needing to have sufficient momentum to reach and traverse the
147
muon system and to satisfy the hardware trigger requirements. At low q2, the track and
148
muon reconstruction requirements favour decays with θl away from 0 and π, where one
149
muon is soft in the B+ rest frame. At intermediate and high q2, the hardware trigger
150
requirements on the pT of the muon candidates favour events away from θl ∼π/2, where
151
the maximum muon momentum tends to be smaller.
152
The simulation is generated with an SM-like q2 distribution [30], and potential variations
153
of the efficiency profile within each q2 interval are considered as a source of systematic
154
uncertainty. The J/ψ veto removes, at a fixed q2 value, a narrow region of cos θl, which
155
cannot be modelled by a polynomial. The efficiency is multiplied by a discontinuous
156
function to account for this effect.
157

158

159
function. The tail parameters are fixed from fits to simulation. In the fits to data, an
160
offset in the position of the peak and a scale factor on the width of the distribution, due

Results
231
The π+µ+µ−mass and cos θl distributions of selected B+ →J/ψπ+ candidates are shown
232
in Fig. 3. Excellent agreement is found between the data and the result of the fit, indicating
233
that the signal efficiency is well described by the corrected simulation. The FH and AFB
234
parameters are found to be consistent with zero, as expected for these decays. Similarly,
235
FH and AFB determined from the fit to the B+ →J/ψK+sample are found to be consistent
236
with zero.
237
The π+µ+µ−mass and cos θl distributions of the selected candidates in the two q2
238
regions are shown in Fig. 4. The data are described well by the result of the fit. The
239
discontinuity in cos θl (visible in the 1.1 < q2 < 6.0 GeV2/c4 range) arises from the J/ψ
240
veto. The observed signal yields are 89 ± 13 and 135 ± 15 events in the low- and high-q2
241
regions, respectively. The uncertainties quoted on the yields are purely statistical and
242
are determined by repeating the fit on pseudodata generated by bootstrapping the data
243
set [42]. Figure 5 shows the one-dimensional cos θl distributions within the mass interval
244
5260 < m(π+µ+µ−) < 5300 MeV/c2, corresponding to a window of approximately twice
245
the π+µ+µ−mass resolution around the known B+ meson mass.
246
The values of AFB and FH for the two q2 intervals are given in Table 2 and are shown
247
in Fig. 6. Two-dimensional confidence interval regions for AFB and FH are shown in
248
Fig. 7. At low q2, the data show a non-zero AFB value and a large value of FH that are
249
not consistent with the SM point within 95% confidence level (CL), but are consistent
250
within 99% CL. At high q2, the data show values of AFB and FH that are consistent with
251
the SM point within 68% CL.
